Where it runs

Not a simulator.

Hackathon circuits are queued to an IBM superconducting processor — transmons on a heavy-hex lattice, held near 15 millikelvin. This is the shape of one.

Heavy-hex coupling map · every qubit has at most 3 neighbours

Bell state · H then CNOT · 1024 shots

|00⟩0
|01⟩0
|10⟩0
|11⟩0

Two qubits, entangled. Neither one has a value of its own — but they always agree. The stray counts in the middle two rows are readout error, and on real hardware they never quite reach zero.

Questions

Before you register.

Anything missing, mail the organising team — the address is at the bottom.

Who can attend?

Any IIT Bombay student, any department, any year — undergraduate, postgraduate or PhD. Access for participants from outside campus will be confirmed alongside the schedule.

Do I need a physics background?

No. Quantum101 assumes complex numbers and a Python loop. Everything else is built from there.

How much Python do I need?

Enough to write a function and a loop. If you have used NumPy once, you are comfortably ahead.

Do I really get to use a quantum computer?

Yes. A free IBM Quantum account puts your circuits on real superconducting processors. You will see the noise, the queue and the counts for yourself.

Can I come to only some sessions?

Yes. Each session stands on its own. The hackathon assumes Quantum101, so do that one first if you can.

What should I bring?

A laptop with Python and Qiskit installed, and an IBM Quantum account. Both links are in Resources.

Is it free?

Every session is free. Places at the hackathon and the lab visits may be limited by room capacity, so registration order will matter for those.
